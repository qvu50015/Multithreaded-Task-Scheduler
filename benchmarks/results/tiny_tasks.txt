Tiny Task Benchmark
===================

- 100,000 tasks
- Each task performs: value * 2
- 5 runs per configuration
- Timing unit: microseconds (us)
- All configurations produced the correct result:
  9,999,900,000

Analysis:
The ThreadPool performs significantly worse than the sequential
implementation for extremely small tasks.

The sequential workload completes in 251.2 us, while the ThreadPool
takes 165,621 us with 1 worker and 321,037 us with 8 workers.

Increasing the number of workers increases the overhead rather than
improving performance. This demonstrates that task scheduling,
synchronization, queue management, and future/result handling can
dominate the cost of very small tasks.

Conclusion:
The ThreadPool is effective for sufficiently large CPU-bound tasks,
but extremely fine-grained tasks are too small to amortize the
overhead of scheduling them individually.

The ThreadPool has to perform a huge amount of infrastructure work for 100,000 individual tasks:

create future
      ↓
create packaged_task
      ↓
queue task
      ↓
lock mutex
      ↓
notify worker
      ↓
worker wakes
      ↓
lock mutex
      ↓
remove task
      ↓
execute tinyWork()
      ↓
store result
